# Glossary

The words this project uses for its own parts, and the ones to avoid.
Orientation and build instructions are in the root [`README.md`](../README.md).

**Nav**:
The row of five tabs in the site header, in three Nav Groups: Research (Fact Bank, Papers), Policy (Brief, Landscape) and Government action (one tab, labelled Enforcement and Legislation, which holds the four sections Legislation, Enforcement, Cases and All). The site title links home to About, which is the front page: a short introduction, then a Research, a Policy and a Government action section, each opening with the question it answers and then what the dashboard offers for it (the Government action section was added on 2026-11-12, when the owner asked for About to carry the day's work: its copy is drafted for the owner to edit, where the intro, Research and Policy are the owner's own; the page opened on three door cards, one per tab, until that day). It was a flat row for a day (2026-11-11) and went back to groups at the owner's request, with Fact Bank ahead of Papers so that each group reads alphabetically. Legislation left the Policy group for a group of its own on 2026-11-12, also at the owner's request, and for an afternoon was two tabs, Bills and Use Cases, until Use cases went back into it as the third view of its toggle and was then removed altogether at the owner's request (it had been one tab with a toggle of three views before); that evening the group was named Government action and its first tab Legislation (the tab had been called Bills). Agenda, a placeholder, was added first in the Policy group the same day, also at the owner's request, and Enforcement, another, in the Government action group at the owner's suggestion that enforcement actions and key cases could have a tab of their own. On 2026-11-17 the owner preferred one tab for Cases, Enforcement and Legislation to the two tabs, Enforcement and Legislation, so the group became a single tab, labelled Enforcement and Legislation in the owner's words (the group label Government action sits under it), with a switch between three sections (see Government action). On 2026-11-23 her reviewers asked for Legislation before Enforcement, so the tab is now labelled Legislation and Enforcement and its switch read All, Legislation, Enforcement, Cases that day; later the same day she asked for All to come last, and it now reads Legislation, Enforcement, Cases, All (All is still where the tab opens).
_Avoid_: section (when you mean a tab)

**Nav Group**:
One of the three clusters in the site header: **Research** (Fact Bank, Papers; Soft Green), **Policy** (Brief, Landscape; Warm Pink) and **Government action** (one tab, labelled Enforcement and Legislation; Warm Gold). A group's tabs sit above a rule and a small uppercase label in the group's accent colour (`data-group`, `--group-accent` in `src/styles/shell.css`). The About page's three sections take the same accents as a rule above each part, and the intro underlines the two unknowns that Research and Policy answer in their colours (`data-group` in `src/styles/policy.css`). A group needs a `--group-accent` line of its own in `shell.css` (without one it takes the green default; a test checks that each has one). A nav group is a label only: it has no view key, no route, and no state. The Economy group (Data Tracker, Job Displacement) is retired, see below.
_Avoid_: tab category, section (when you mean the group)

**Brief** (the tab; `#brief`, and `#brief/<section>` for one part of it; called **Agenda** until 2026-11-19):
The first tab of the Policy group (`#agendaArea`; alphabetical, so ahead of Landscape). It was a "Coming soon" placeholder named Agenda from 2026-11-12 until 2026-11-19, when the owner asked for Becky Chao's paper to be added "engaging and interactive" and then had the tab renamed Brief (Agenda promised a list of the team's commitments, which this page is not). Its content is an excerpt of the April 2026 paper *Ideas for Shared Economic Prosperity in the AI Transition*, drawn from `data/agenda-data.js` by `src/js/35-agenda.js`. Internal names are unchanged, as the Landscape's still say policy: the view key `agenda`, `#agendaArea`, `agenda.css`, `AGENDA_DATA`; the old addresses `#agenda` and `#agenda/<section>` still work and land on `#brief`. The words are the brief's, character for character (read from the PDF and checked against a second extraction, nothing missing and nothing extra; a test pins a fingerprint of them), except that the owner had the whole introduction, the conclusion and the notes only they cited (1 to 13) left out on 2026-11-19, so the page's footnotes start at 14; what is ours is the link to the full paper, the short pathway labels, the "On the Landscape" pointers and the opening line each closed idea shows. The masthead (`TAB_DESCS.agenda`) asks "How do we make sure AI benefits everyone?" and its standfirst, the owner's own sentence, holds the one link in any masthead: the brief's title, to the full paper on the Economic Security Project's website (`url`). It says the page is pulled from the brief and that below are its pathways (counted from the data), from its first to its last. The page is the ideas alone: the four pathways with their ideas as accordion rows, and the Sources list. It has no nav: the owner had four overview cards, a "How this page is built" note, a sticky bar of jump pills, a note box saying it was an excerpt (its words became the standfirst's), the introduction, the conclusion and an Expand all ideas button taken out on 2026-11-19. The policy agenda that sat above the Landscape list until 2026-11-12 was deleted that day and is not this.
_Avoid_: Agenda (its old name, still in the internal names), Paper (the tab shows an excerpt of the paper), Report

**Pathway** (Brief; `pathways` in `data/agenda-data.js`):
One of the brief's four headings, each with two ideas: (1) Modernize the Social Contract for an AI-Driven Economy, (2) Protect & Empower Workers, (3) Shape Fair and Competitive AI Markets, (4) Mitigate AI's Role in Exacerbating the Affordability Crisis. The brief's own introduction lists them in this order, and they are the Landscape's four areas in the Landscape's order (Economic Security, Labor & Worker Rights, AI Governance, Mitigating Harms), so each wears its area's colour. Our short names for the pills are Social contract, Workers, Markets and Affordability.
_Avoid_: Section (a section of the page is any of introduction, pathway, conclusion or sources), Area (that is the Landscape's word for the same four)

**Idea** (Brief; `ideas` in a pathway):
One of the brief's eight second-level headings, drawn as an accordion row: closed it shows the title and the brief's own first sentence, open it shows the text, and the first idea's four "emerging ideas" (the brief's bold-led list) as items. Not a Policy: an idea is the brief's, a policy is a card on the Landscape.
_Avoid_: Policy, Proposal, Recommendation

**Source drawer** (Brief; `.ag-source`):
What opens under a paragraph when a footnote number in it is pressed: that note, with every web address linked. One is open at a time; Escape or Close shuts it. Not a tooltip, because a tooltip cannot be reached by keyboard or pressed reliably on a phone. The Sources list at the end holds every note the page cites (12 to 57, as the brief numbers them), each with a "See in the text" link back to its passage.
_Avoid_: Popover, Tooltip, Footnote (the footnote is the note itself)

**On the Landscape** (Brief; `related` in `data/agenda-data.js`):
The row of buttons under an idea (or under each of the first idea's four emerging ideas) that open the policy cards on the Landscape covering the same ground. Ours, not the brief's: drafted by Claude on 2026-11-19 from the cards' titles and summaries, to be reviewed by the owner, and said so in the note at the foot of the page. A card that is archived or renamed drops out of its row; an idea with no card (the data-centre energy costs one, on 2026-11-19) has no row.

**Landscape** (the tab; `#policy`):
The second tab in the Policy group, after the Brief (Legislation was the second until 2026-11-12): the list of policies (`#policyArea`). It had a North Star agenda above the list until 2026-11-12, when the agenda was removed. Its route is still `#policy` (and `#policy/<id>` opens a policy card), so old links keep working; only the nav label changed. Not to be confused with the `landscape` field on a policy card, which describes the bills and initiatives around that one policy. Since 2026-11-19 it has three views, switched with the **All**, **Links** and **Compare** buttons under the masthead (the owner asked for all policies, how the policies relate to one another, and Compare; featured policies are a label in the All view, not a view); the views have no routes of their own, as the old Map and Links buttons had none.
_Avoid_: Policy tab, Map (the old names)

**Government action** (the group's one tab, labelled Enforcement and Legislation; `#viewGovernment`):
The one nav tab of the Government action group, since 2026-11-17: the owner preferred one tab for Cases, Enforcement and Legislation to the two tabs, Enforcement and Legislation, they had been. It holds four Sections, each a view of its own with an address of its own (`#legislation`, `#enforcement`, `#cases`, `#all`, in the switch's order), under ONE masthead for the tab (the owner, 2026-11-18: "There shouldn't be a different header and blurb for each view"; see Masthead below), switched by four buttons at the start of the toolbar (`#govSwitch` in `src/views/60-legislation.html`: Legislation, Enforcement, Cases, All: the owner's reviewers asked on 2026-11-23 for Legislation, then Enforcement, Cases follows Enforcement because the two are the agencies' and the courts' side of the same question, and the owner then asked for All to come last; it was All, Legislation, Enforcement, Cases that morning, and before that All and then alphabetical, Cases, Enforcement, Legislation), with the Show menu right after them. The nav tab is marked for all four and opens the section that was open last (Legislation at first, since 2026-11-24, when the owner asked to "Have legislation be the default view for the government action page"; All from 2026-11-18, and Legislation before that). All holds everything and the other three hold their own data alone, on a map shaded by it, with a card that holds it alone. Each section is a view in the router (`VIEWS`), so the body carries the section's own `-view` class and there is no class for the tab itself. The place chosen in the Show menu is shared by the four (`legView.pinned`). The tab is labelled Enforcement and Legislation (the owner's words, 2026-11-18; it read Legislation and Enforcement on 2026-11-23, when her reviewers asked for Legislation first, and the owner put it back on 2026-11-24) above the group label Government action; the label leaves out All and Cases. About describes it as its Government action section and links to each section, in the switch's order, All last.
_Avoid_: tab (for a section); the Enforcement tab and the Legislation tab (they were tabs until 2026-11-17)

**All** (a section of Government action; `#all`):
The last section in the Government action tab's switch (since 2026-11-23, when the owner asked for Legislation, Enforcement, Cases, then All; it was first before) and, from 2026-11-18 until 2026-11-24, the one the tab opened on (the owner, 2026-11-18: "There should be an All view that's separate from the Legislation view. Currently it looks like legislation holds all the data"; on 2026-11-24 she made Legislation the default again). It holds everything, in the owner's order ("bills, and then cases and enforcement"), in its card and on its page: the bills (the Legislation table), the key cases and the enforcement actions (each list ten to a page). (The 2026 midterm results, a table at the head of the page and a block in the card, came first until the owner had them removed on 2026-11-23.) It shows the bills' controls (Status, the checkbox, the topic chips, the search, Sort), and the search and the chips narrow the cases and enforcement actions too, since a row's words and topics are the same vocabulary as a bill's; the place is shared with the other sections and narrows every list. Its map is shaded by items, the bills, key cases and enforcement actions added up per state (the one place they are counted together, said in the legend: actions and cases are otherwise never counted with the bills or with each other), in thirds of the states that have any; its state card has a block for each kind, key cases then enforcement actions, each ending with a "See more details below" link that pins the place and takes the reader to that list, wherever the place has rows of the kind (a place with none says None listed and has no link). Its Federal entry in the Show menu is "Federal (Congress, agencies and courts)". Its Export to CSV pill saves all three kinds in one file (see Export to CSV). Its address is `#all`, with no place. Legislation, Enforcement and Cases show their own data alone.
_Avoid_: Overview, Home (it is a section, not a front page; About is the front page)

**Masthead** (the Government action tab's, `TAB_DESCS` in `src/js/70-render.js`; `govLede()` and `govMasthead()` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`):
The header and blurb at the top of the Government action tab. There is ONE for all four sections, the same whichever is open (the owner, 2026-11-18: "There shouldn't be a different header and blurb for each view"). The header is the question "What is government doing about AI?". The blurb is computed from the data: the active bills, the key cases and the enforcement actions, each counted on its own (words for small counts, digits once the largest is big), "about AI, federal and in the states", and then how to use the page: "Use the switch to see everything together or one kind at a time, and hover a state or choose a place in the Show menu to narrow what you see." It does not change with the section or the filters. The cautions the Enforcement and Cases blurbs used to carry (an allegation is not a finding; a ruling is only what that court held) are in the note under each list's table.
_Avoid_: a masthead for each section

**Reset button** (`#legReset` on Government action, `#ppReset` on Papers, `#kfReset` on the Fact Bank; the shared look is `.reset-btn` in `src/styles/filterbar.css`):
The one way to clear a tab's search, order and filters (the owner, 2026-11-18, chose it from three sketches because the small Clear pill and the Clear filters link were hard to see; they are gone from all three tabs). It is always there at the right end of the toolbar, the same size as the Sort menu, solid navy with the count of what differs from how the tab opens ("Reset all (4)") and an outline otherwise ("Reset all"). Pressing it puts the whole tab back. On Government action: no jurisdiction, no filter in any section, the order back to Most recent, and the Legislation view, where the tab opens (it was All until 2026-11-24); the count is the jurisdiction, the search words, the status, each topic, the checkbox, the order if not Most recent, and the section if not Legislation. On Papers: the search, the order (Newest first) and Methodology, Category and Region; on the Fact Bank: the search, the order (Most recent), Evidence, both rating minimums, each keyword and Foundational. Pressing it with nothing to reset says so (a live region), rather than doing nothing silently.
_Avoid_: Clear, Clear filters (the old controls), Sort (it is not a filter, but Reset does put it back)

**Legislation** (a section of Government action; `#legislation`):
The first section in the Government action tab's switch (`#legislationArea`; since 2026-11-23, ahead of Enforcement, Cases and All; it was second, after All, that morning; the tab opened on it until 2026-11-18, when All became the section it opened on, and opens on it again since 2026-11-24; its route has always been `#legislation`; it was a tab of its own until 2026-11-17, and until 2026-11-12 the second tab of the Policy group, for part of that day labelled Bills): a map of the United States, with a card beside it, and a table of legislation under it. The map, the card and the controls above them (the toolbar, the topic chips, the search) are shared with the other three sections: one area, `#legislationArea` (see Government action). Pointing at a state shows its bills in the card (which holds the bills alone here: the results and what the other sections list are on All); clicking it, or choosing it in the Show menu, pins it and narrows the table. The states carry no letters of their own (pointing at one names it in the card), and the nine too small to point at each also have a small-state button beside the map. A bill is state or federal: Congress is the Federal entry of the Show menu and works like picking a state, and since 2026-11-23 it is also a Federal button on the map, with a States button (every state at once) beside it since 2026-11-24 (see Federal button and States button). The map is shaded by bills, in three steps, the thirds of the states that have bills, with the legend naming each range; which bills it shades follows the place pinned (Federal shades nothing, since a federal bill is on no state, and a single pinned state shades only itself; see Federal button). The 2026 midterm results are not on this page, or on any section, any more: they were a layer of the map until 2026-11-18, then a block in the card, totals in its overview and a table at the head of All, and the owner had them removed on 2026-11-23 (see `legislation-sources.md`). The bills are in `data/legislation-bills.js`, which `tools/legislation.py` generates (see [`legislation-sources.md`](legislation-sources.md)) and the page fetches the first time any of the four sections opens (the card in all of them counts from it, and the map on Legislation and All is shaded from it), adding the rows to `LEGISLATION_DATA`. Federal: our own list, found by `tools/congress_fetch.py` in GovInfo's bill-status data (public domain): every bill and joint resolution of the 119th Congress whose title names AI or a close relative of it, each with its official record (title, stage, latest action, committees) and the Congressional Research Service's summary where it has written one (else its official title); a bill whose title does not but whose CRS summary does is hidden behind the checkbox, as a state bill that only mentions AI in its text is, and a House bill and its identical Senate companion share a row. State: our own list, from Open States (which follows each legislature's own record): every bill since 2025 whose text mentions "artificial intelligence" (and, once their searches are finished, "deepfake", "digital replica" or "synthetic media"; `TERMS` in `tools/openstates_fetch.py`), shown with its official title, the legislature's own summary where it gives one, its subject terms, its latest action and a link to the legislature's page. Nothing is taken from NCSL. Bills that failed, were withdrawn or vetoed, or whose session ended without passing them are left out (a note says how many). A bill whose title and summary do not name AI, and which only mentions it somewhere in its text, stays out of every count, shading and table until the checkbox "Include bills that only lightly mention AI", beside the Status menu, is on; the bill then shows marked "Mentions AI only in its text". The toolbar's Status menu and that checkbox, the topic chips (always showing, above the search) and the search and Sort narrow everything counted on Legislation and on All (where they narrow the key cases and enforcement actions too): the map's shading, the card, the counts and the table, while the masthead keeps saying what is loaded (on Enforcement and Cases they are not shown and do not apply). The table is sorted by most recent last action unless Sort says oldest or jurisdiction. Under the table's title (the jurisdiction and the count, "New York · 8 bills", with no word Bills in it) a stats bar says where the bills in view stand (see Stats bar). The bills table shows ten to a page (`LEG_PAGE_SIZE` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`), with the pager the Papers list uses; the card beside the map gives the jurisdiction's stats (its bill count, and the stats bar's bar and each stage's count and share) and a link, "See more details below", that pins the jurisdiction and takes the reader to its table (only the cards that hold bills, on Legislation and All, have it, and only for a place that has bills: none where there is no data yet to show); it never grows taller than the map column, scrolling inside if it must (it lists no bills: the owner had the bill previews taken out of the cards on 2026-11-12). Puerto Rico, the one territory Open States covers, has bills but no shape on the map: the Show menu and the tables reach it. The state outlines are generated into `data/us-map.js` by `tools/us_map.py`. The masthead is the tab's, the same in every section (`govLede()` and `govMasthead()`). There is no About door.

**Enforcement** (a section of Government action; `#enforcement`):
The second section in the Government action tab's switch (`#enforcementArea`, the area it shares with Cases and All): what agencies and attorneys general have done about AI. It lists the rows of `data/legislation-actions.js` (`LEGISLATION_ACTIONS`, generated from the Google Sheet's `enforcement` and `cases` tabs by `tools/actions.py`, never by hand; the row format is at the top of that file, the strict rules are in `content/README.md`, and `src/js/55-enforcement.js` draws it) whose kind is `enforcement`. It began as a placeholder tab on 2026-11-12 (the owner's suggestion that enforcement actions and key cases could have a tab of their own), was built on 2026-11-17 as "two tabs by kind" (Legislation: the bills, and until 2026-11-23 the midterm results; Enforcement: the agencies, attorneys general and courts), and became one section of the one tab that evening, at the owner's request, with the courts' rulings in a section of their own, Cases. While the file has no row of the kind the section is still the placeholder (one note says nothing is here yet; the masthead is the tab's and says nothing of it); the first row turns the list on, and the list, its count and the tab's masthead are then said from the rows. The first 14 rows went in on 2026-11-17, in one batch: each was checked against the agency's or court's own page, and none has yet been read by someone with legal training, which the file's own rule requires before an entry is live (a comment above the rows says so). The list has its own count, never added to the bills or to the cases, newest first; a status and an outcome are plain text, and each row links to the agency's or attorney general's own page. A Show menu, a search and topic chips (the same topics as the bills) narrow it. The PLACE is shared by the three sections: the Show menu is the Legislation menu, a place chosen in any is still chosen in the others (`legView.pinned`), and the address carries it (`#enforcement/texas`, `#cases/federal`; a place that is not one shows every jurisdiction and the address is rewritten). "Federal" is two bodies, so Legislation's entry reads "Federal (Congress)" and here it reads "Federal (agencies and courts)". The map's card on All gets a block for each kind (key cases, then enforcement actions: the count for the place, "None listed" never "none", no titles, and a "See more details below" link to that list wherever the place has rows), and on Enforcement it holds that block alone, but only once the file has a row. The section does not ask for the bills file. The About page's Government action paragraph says what the sections list; it changed with the first rows.

**Cases** (a section of Government action; `#cases`):
The third section in the Government action tab's switch, after Legislation and Enforcement and ahead of All (it shares `#enforcementArea` and `src/js/55-enforcement.js` with Enforcement; the order is Legislation, Enforcement, Cases, All since 2026-11-23, when the owner's reviewers asked for Legislation, then Enforcement, and the owner then asked for All last; it was All, Legislation, Enforcement, Cases that morning, and All and then alphabetical, Cases, Enforcement, Legislation, before): what courts have decided or are deciding about AI, the rows of `data/legislation-actions.js` whose kind is `case` (a ruling, or a case still being decided, such as Mobley v. Workday; a government's own suit, such as the FTC's against Evolv, is an enforcement action, not a case). It works as Enforcement does, with its own count, its own placeholder note ("Nothing is here yet. Key cases will be listed here."), a note under its table that says a ruling is only what that court held, and the same Show menu, search and topic chips; a topic picked on Enforcement that Cases has no row for is dropped on the way across. Its address is `#cases` and `#cases/<place>`.
_Avoid_: Key cases as the section's name (that is the list's title, "All jurisdictions · 1 key case")

**Small-state button** (`#legTiles`, `.leg-tile`):
One of nine buttons beside the Legislation map, one for each state whose outline is narrower than 45 map units (`LEG_SMALL` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`): Vermont, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland and DC, north to south. It wears its state's colour and does what the state's shape does: pointing at it, or giving it the keyboard's focus, previews the state in the panel, and clicking it pins the state. It sits in a column at the map's right edge, above the Federal and States buttons at the map's bottom right, and in a labelled row under the map on a phone (520 px and narrower), after them. Which states get one is computed from the outlines, not listed by hand. The map's own states carry no letters: a label that fits one shape (Florida's) sat off-centre on another, and the panel already names a state when it is pointed at.
_Avoid_: tile or callout (the markup says tile; in prose, small-state button), label (the map has none), abbreviation on the map

**Topic** (Legislation filter; `LEGISLATION_TOPICS` in `data/legislation-data.js`):
One of the nineteen headings of the Legislation tab's filter chips (Hiring and work, Deepfakes and likeness, Federal preemption and so on), the places AI is used first and how it is governed after. It is our reading, not a legislature's or anyone else's list: the page assigns a topic when one of its words is in a bill's title, or two different words of it are in the bill's summary or subject terms. A bill can have several, and "No topic" finds those with none. The chips carry no counts (the owner, 2026-11-24: "Remove counts from topics in the Government Action tab"); a chip that would leave nothing, with the search and the other filters applied, is dimmed. Not to be confused with the Fact Bank's keywords.
_Avoid_: category (the Papers tab's word for a different list), tag, keyword (the Fact Bank's word)

**Federal button** (`#legFedBtn`, `.leg-level`; added 2026-11-23 at the owner's reviewers' request; moved to the map's bottom right on 2026-11-24, when the States button joined it):
A button on the Government action map that selects Federal as a place: a Capitol dome drawn inline in the button's own colour (navy) over the word Federal. It sits with the States button in one group at the map's bottom right (`#legLevels`, side by side, the owner's placing of 2026-11-24; the small-state buttons stay in the gutter at the map's right edge), and in a row under the map, at its right, on a phone (520 px and narrower). Congress is a place like a state, so it is one more `data-state` button (`data-state="federal"`, the key `LEG_FED` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`) handled by the same code as a state's shape or small-state button: pointing at it or giving it the keyboard's focus previews Federal in the card, pressing it pins Federal (the Show menu, the table's title and count, the card's stats, the lists and the address of Enforcement and Cases all follow), pressing it again lets go, and Reset clears it. It is a real `<button>` with `aria-pressed`; pinned it is filled navy and ringed (a shape and a fill, not a colour alone). Its accessible name is the Show menu's Federal entry for the open section (Federal (Congress) on Legislation, Federal (agencies and courts) on Enforcement and Cases, Federal (Congress, agencies and courts) on All), which begins with the visible word. **It also decides what the map shades** (the owner, 2026-11-24: "Federal button defaults to highlighting all legislation on the map. Please correct it to highlight only federal legislation and enforcement actions. It's likely that nothing will be highlighted"): with Federal pinned only the federal rows count, and a federal row is on no state, so no state and no small-state button is coloured and the legend is the one line of how many federal rows there are ("Federal bills, not on the map 286"); it shades nothing on every section. Pointing at it only previews it. A single state pinned shades only that state (the owner, 2026-11-24: "pinning a single state also shade only that state"), in the shade it has with nothing pinned (the steps are still every state's), with the legend reduced to one line about it ("Texas 5 bills"; a small state's button is coloured too; a territory has no shape, so it shades nothing and is counted apart); letting go brings every state back. It is not a small state and sits outside the Small states group.
_Avoid_: Federal tile, Capitol button (the word on the button is Federal)

**States button** (`#legStatesBtn`, `.leg-level`; added 2026-11-24 at the owner's request: "Add another button on the map that says 'States'"):
The other level of government's button on the Government action map, beside Federal at the map's bottom right: four tiles drawn inline in the button's own colour (navy) over the word States. It selects every state at once as a place, `data-state="states"` (the key `LEG_STATES` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`), the same place as the Show menu's **All states** entry (first in its States group). It works as Federal and a state do: pointing at it previews All states in the card, pressing it pins the place (the table's title and count, "All states · 496 bills", the card's stats and its link to the details below, the lists and the address of Enforcement and Cases, `#enforcement/states` and `#cases/states`, all follow), pressing it again lets go, and Reset clears it; the Show menu and the two buttons are one choice. A place is asked with `legPlaceIn(place, scope)` (a bill's place is `federal` or its state's code, a row's is `enfPlaceOf`): States is every place that is not federal, a state or a territory (which has no shape on the map), so Federal and States together are every jurisdiction and their counts add up (on 2026-11-24, 286 federal bills and 496 state bills, 782 in all). It shades what the map always shades, the states' own rows, so the map looks as it does with nothing pinned, without the legend's federal line, which is not in view. Its accessible name is All states.
_Avoid_: Territories button (a territory has no shape and is reached through the Show menu's Territories group, and States includes the territories' rows), Level buttons (the markup's word; in prose, the Federal and States buttons)

**Status order** (the order of progress; `rank` in `LEGISLATION_STAGES`, `data/legislation-data.js`; added 2026-11-23):
How far along each stage is, and what the bills table's Status column sorts by: Introduced 1, In committee 2, Passed one chamber 3, Passed both chambers 4, Enacted 5, and Failed 0 (below every stage that is alive; failed bills are never listed, so the rank only keeps the rule whole). The `rank` of each entry is the one place the order is kept: the Status column's sort, the Status menu and the stats bar all read it (`legStageRank` and `legStageKeys` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`), so none of them can drift into alphabetical order, which would put Enacted before In committee. **Passed chamber** (`passed` on a bill row; added 2026-11-23):
Which chamber passed a bill that has passed one, or which two chambers passed one that has passed both, and the day each did, shown in the Status column under the stage as "Passed House, September 30, 2026", or, for a bill that passed both, both in the order they passed it: "Passed Senate, May 19, 2026; Assembly, August 30, 2026" (the owner, 2026-11-23: "Specify which chambers in the government action tab"). It is written by the generator tools from the bills' own actions (`passages_of` in `tools/congress_fetch.py` for a federal bill, `os_passages` in `tools/legislation.py` for a state bill) and only for a bill at the one-chamber or both-chambers stage. The order is decided there, once (the earlier date first, the lower chamber first on the same day), and the page and the CSV show it as written. The chamber is the source's own word: House or Senate for a federal bill, and for a state bill the legislature's own name for the chamber, which is the Assembly, not the House, in California, New York, New Jersey, Nevada and Wisconsin. A chamber that passed a bill more than once gives its latest date, the vote that stands. Nothing is guessed: a bill with no chamber or no date in its source has no `passed`, and the page then says "Which chamber passed it, and when, is not in our data." ("Which chambers passed it, and when, is not in our data." for a bill at the both-chambers stage); a bill at that stage with only one of its two in the data shows that one and "The other chamber and its date are not in our data." A committee's vote is not a chamber's passage and is not named (California's SCR 82: Open States classes the Assembly's two committee votes as passage, so only its Senate is shown, under a stage that still says both chambers, which the stage rule decides and this does not change). The search finds it ("passed assembly", "passed house", "march 2 2026"; a bill that passed both is found by either chamber and either date), and the CSV export has it as two columns, the chambers and the dates as YYYY-MM-DD, each joined with "; " in the same order when there are two.

**Export to CSV** (`#legExport`, `.leg-export`; added 2026-11-23):
The pill at the right of the Bills table's title that saves what the section lists as a CSV file: every bill in view, all of them, not the ten on the page, with the jurisdiction, the search, the Status menu, the topics, the checkbox and the order applied, one row each under a header row. The bills' columns are the table's with the bill number, the session, the chambers and dates it passed, the topics and the link written out; the cases' and actions' are who acted, the date of the action or ruling, the outcome, the day it was last checked, our description (as the Summary), the topics, the host as the Source and the link.
