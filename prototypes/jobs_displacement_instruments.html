Where AI displacement would show up first — and whether it has

No single statistic can tell you whether AI is displacing workers. So this page runs a battery of separate tests, each watching a different place the effect would surface. Every test reports what it found and how much it could have found: a quiet reading from a blunt, slow instrument is very weak evidence, and is marked as such. Nothing here is a verdict on the labor market.

Before the tests: what this evidence cannot see

Displacement arrives as non-hiring

A firm that quietly stops opening a role produces no layoff, no unemployment claim, and no separation. An entire cohort can be shut out while every headline series reads normal.

Reallocation looks like nothing

If AI destroys work in one place and creates it in another, aggregate employment is flat. The aggregate tests read null even though the labor market has churned underneath them.

These tests are not independent

Several draw on the same CPS microdata and will move together for reasons that have nothing to do with AI. Counting how many are quiet measures nothing, so no score is published here.

The instruments lag

Quarterly series, subject to revision, describe a labor market of six to eighteen months ago. A null today is a statement about the recent past, not the present.

The tests

Ordered fastest and sharpest first. The blunt aggregates come last, because they are the last place displacement would appear.

How to read this page

Tests are added as they are built, and every reading is regenerated when the underlying data refreshes. A test that is quiet today is not settled; it is a detector that has not tripped, with a stated threshold for what would trip it. The methods page carries the estimating equations, samples and known failure modes for each one.
