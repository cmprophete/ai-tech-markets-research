# Glossary

The words this project uses for its own parts, and the ones to avoid.
Orientation and build instructions are in the root [`README.md`](../README.md).

**Nav**:
The row of six tabs in the site header, in three labelled Nav Groups: Research (Fact Bank, Papers), Policy (Agenda, Landscape) and Government action (Enforcement, Legislation). The site title links home to About, which is the front page: a short introduction, then a Research, a Policy and a Government action section, each opening with the question it answers and then what the dashboard offers for it (the Government action section was added on 2026-11-12, when the owner asked for About to carry the day's work: its copy is drafted for the owner to edit, where the intro, Research and Policy are the owner's own; the page opened on three door cards, one per tab, until that day). It was a flat row for a day (2026-11-11) and went back to groups at the owner's request, with Fact Bank ahead of Papers so that each group reads alphabetically. Legislation left the Policy group for a group of its own on 2026-11-12, also at the owner's request, and for an afternoon was two tabs, Bills and Use Cases, until Use cases went back into it as the third view of its toggle and was then removed altogether at the owner's request (it had been one tab with a toggle of three views before); that evening the group was named Government action and its first tab Legislation (the tab had been called Bills). Agenda, a placeholder, was added first in the Policy group the same day, also at the owner's request, and Enforcement, another, in the Government action group at the owner's suggestion that enforcement actions and key cases could have a tab of their own.
_Avoid_: section (when you mean a tab)

**Nav Group**:
One of the three labelled clusters in the site header: **Research** (Fact Bank, Papers; Soft Green), **Policy** (Agenda, Landscape; Warm Pink) and **Government action** (Enforcement, Legislation; Warm Gold). A group's tabs sit above a rule and a small uppercase label in the group's accent colour (`data-group`, `--group-accent` in `src/styles/shell.css`). The About page's three sections take the same accents as a rule above each part, and the intro underlines the two unknowns that Research and Policy answer in their colours (`data-group` in `src/styles/policy.css`). A group needs a `--group-accent` line of its own in `shell.css` (without one it takes the green default; a test checks that each has one). A nav group is a label only: it has no view key, no route, and no state. The Economy group (Data Tracker, Job Displacement) is retired, see below.
_Avoid_: tab category, section (when you mean the group)

**Agenda** (the tab; `#agenda`):
A placeholder, the first tab of the Policy group (`#agendaArea`; alphabetical, so ahead of Landscape), added 2026-11-12 at the owner's request. Nothing is behind it: the masthead (`TAB_DESCS.agenda`) asks "What is our policy agenda?" and says "Coming soon.", and one note under it says nothing is here yet and points to Landscape. Replace the note and the masthead copy when there is an agenda. The policy agenda that sat above the Landscape list until 2026-11-12 was deleted from the repository that day; none of it is shown on this tab.

**Landscape** (the tab; `#policy`):
The second tab in the Policy group, after the Agenda placeholder (Legislation was the second until 2026-11-12): the list of policies (`#policyArea`). It had a North Star agenda above the list until 2026-11-12, when the agenda was removed. Its route is still `#policy` (and `#policy/<id>` opens a policy card), so old links keep working; only the nav label changed. Not to be confused with the `landscape` field on a policy card, which describes the bills and initiatives around that one policy.
_Avoid_: Policy tab, Map (the old names)

**Legislation** (the tab; `#legislation`):
The second tab of the Government action group (`#legislationArea`; alphabetical, after Enforcement; its route has always been `#legislation`, and until 2026-11-12 it was the second tab of the Policy group; for part of that day the tab was labelled Bills): a map of the United States, a table of legislation and a table of the 2026 midterm results. Pointing at a state shows its legislation and its Senate, House and governor results in a panel; clicking it, or choosing it in the Show menu, pins it and narrows both tables. The states carry no letters of their own (pointing at one names it in the panel), and the nine too small to point at each also have a small-state button beside the map. A bill is state or federal: Congress is the Federal entry of the Show menu and works like picking a state (there is no Federal button). The map has two layers, Bills (shaded in three steps, the thirds of the states that have bills, with the legend naming each range) and 2026 Midterm results (coloured by the Senate, House or governor races, whichever is chosen); it opens on Bills. The toggle at the top switches between those two. Its masthead asks "What is currently on the table?" on Bills and "How did the 2026 midterms go?" on the results. The 2026 Midterm results layer is only about the results: the filter bar and the bills table are out of view, the card beside the map holds no bills, the Show menu offers only the states (Congress and Puerto Rico have no results), and the masthead asks "How did the 2026 midterms go?" and counts the races from the data. The midterm results come from the Associated Press's midterm results and from nothing else: the page is cited below the map, and each state's row links to AP's page for its races. The bills are in `data/legislation-bills.js`, which `tools/legislation.py` generates (see [`legislation-sources.md`](legislation-sources.md)) and the page fetches the first time either tab of the group opens, adding the rows to `LEGISLATION_DATA`. Federal: the list is the Brennan Center for Justice's Artificial Intelligence Legislation Tracker, and each bill's title, stage and latest action are read from its official record at GovInfo. State: our own list, from Open States (which follows each legislature's own record): every bill since 2025 whose text mentions "artificial intelligence" (and, once their searches are finished, "deepfake", "digital replica" or "synthetic media"; `TERMS` in `tools/openstates_fetch.py`), shown with its official title, the legislature's own summary where it gives one, its subject terms, its latest action and a link to the legislature's page. Nothing is taken from NCSL. Bills that failed, were withdrawn or vetoed, or whose session ended without passing them are left out (a note says how many). A bill whose title and summary do not name AI, and which only mentions it somewhere in its text, stays out of every count, shading and table until the checkbox "Include bills that only mention AI", under More filters, is on; the bill then shows marked "Mentions AI only in its text". Under the toolbar a filter bar (search and Sort in view; Status, that checkbox and the topic chips behind More filters) narrows everything counted: the map's shading, the panel, the counts and the table, while the masthead keeps saying what is loaded. The table is sorted by most recent last action unless Sort says oldest or jurisdiction. Under the table's title (the jurisdiction and the count, "New York · 8 bills", with no word Bills in it) a stats bar says where the bills in view stand (see Stats bar). The bills table shows ten to a page (`LEG_PAGE_SIZE` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`), with the pager the Papers list uses; the card beside the map gives the jurisdiction's stats (its bill count, and the stats bar's bar and each stage's count and share) and a link, "See more details below", that pins the jurisdiction and takes the reader to its tables (the Bills title on the Bills layer, the results title on the results layer); it never grows taller than the map column, scrolling inside if it must (it lists no bills: the owner had the bill previews taken out of the cards on 2026-11-12). Puerto Rico, the one territory Open States covers, has bills but no shape on the map: the Show menu and the tables reach it. The state outlines are generated into `data/us-map.js` by `tools/us_map.py`. The masthead's standfirst (`TAB_DESCS.legislation`) says what is loaded, computed from the data. There is no About door.

**Enforcement** (the tab; `#enforcement`):
A placeholder, the first tab of the Government action group (`#enforcementArea`; alphabetical, ahead of Legislation), added 2026-11-12 at the owner's suggestion that enforcement actions and key cases could be a tab of their own. Nothing is behind it: the masthead (`TAB_DESCS.enforcement`) asks "What has been enforced, and what have the courts said?" and says "Coming soon: enforcement actions and key cases.", and one note under it says nothing is here yet and points to Legislation. It does not ask for the bills file. The rows it will list are meant to come from `data/legislation-actions.js` (see [`legislation-sources.md`](legislation-sources.md)); what the tab will show is not decided. Replace the note and the masthead copy when there is something to list.

**Small-state button** (`#legTiles`, `.leg-tile`):
One of nine buttons beside the Legislation map, one for each state whose outline is narrower than 45 map units (`LEG_SMALL` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`): Vermont, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland and DC, north to south. It wears its state's colour and does what the state's shape does: pointing at it, or giving it the keyboard's focus, previews the state in the panel, and clicking it pins the state. It sits in a column at the map's right edge, and in a labelled row under the map on a phone (520 px and narrower). Which states get one is computed from the outlines, not listed by hand. The map's own states carry no letters: a label that fits one shape (Florida's) sat off-centre on another, and the panel already names a state when it is pointed at.
_Avoid_: tile or callout (the markup says tile; in prose, small-state button), label (the map has none), abbreviation on the map

**Topic** (Legislation filter; `LEGISLATION_TOPICS` in `data/legislation-data.js`):
One of the nineteen headings of the Legislation tab's filter chips (Hiring and work, Deepfakes and likeness, Federal preemption and so on), the places AI is used first and how it is governed after. It is our reading, not a legislature's or the Brennan Center's list: the page assigns a topic when one of its words is in a bill's title, or two different words of it are in the bill's summary, subject terms or the Brennan Center's labels. A bill can have several, and "No topic" finds those with none. Not to be confused with a bill's `topics` field, the source's own category labels (the Brennan Center's, shown as "Brennan Center labels"), or the Fact Bank's keywords.
_Avoid_: category (the Papers tab's word for a different list), tag, keyword (the Fact Bank's word)

**Active (bill)**:
A bill that has been introduced and has not failed (the owner's definition, 2026-11-12). Which stages count is the `active` flag on each entry of `LEGISLATION_STAGES` in `data/legislation-data.js`: introduced, in committee, passed one chamber, passed both chambers and enacted are active; failed is not. A bill with no stage cannot be judged and is not listed as active, and a note under the table says how many were left out and why.

**Stats bar** (Legislation tab; `#legBillsStats`, drawn by `legStatsBar()` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`):
The bar under the Bills section's title, added at the owner's request on 2026-11-12. That title is the jurisdiction and the count ("New York · 8 bills") and nothing else: the word Bills was taken out of it the same day, at the owner's request, and the jurisdiction and count were set at the size of a section title (20px bold); once the bills are loaded the count is always there, even as "0 bills". It says where the bills in view stand: one segment for each of the five active stages that has some bills (the stages of the Status menu, in its order: introduced, in committee, passed one chamber, passed both chambers, enacted), as wide as its share of the bills, the bar and its key sharing one line across the table (the owner asked on 2026-11-12 for the bar to run the length of the table and for the text to be on the bar's line): the key at the right on one line and the bar filling the rest, the key wrapping beside the bar on a narrower window and the two stacking below 860px, with the key giving each stage's count and its share of the bills in view as a whole-number percentage ("370 in committee (69%)"; the owner asked for the percentages on 2026-11-12). Each percentage is rounded on its own, so together they can come to a point or two either side of 100, and they are not forced to 100; a share under 1% reads "<1%" and one over 99% with other stages having some ">99%", so a stage with bills never reads as none and one that is not all of them never reads as all. It counts the bills the title counts, so it follows the jurisdiction pinned or chosen in the Show menu, the search, Status, the topic chips and the checkbox for bills that only mention AI, and it is empty (and hidden) when no bill is in view. It always counts all of those bills, not the page of ten the table is showing. A screen reader gets one sentence for the bar ("Where the 8 bills stand: 3 introduced (38%), 4 in committee (50%), 1 enacted (13%).", with "under 1%" and "over 99%" in words); the segments' hover text gives the stage, its count and its percentage. The colours are the map's greens, deepening with each stage, and navy for enacted, with a hairline between segments. It is drawn from the data each time the table is, so nothing in it is typed in.
_Avoid_: progress bar (it is not progress toward anything), chart, funnel

**Economy**:
A retired Nav Group (the middle of the three), holding Data Tracker and Job Displacement (`data-group="economy"`). Named for the question it asks, which the About lede states as "how the economy will absorb it." Formerly called "Adoption," which named only the Census BTOS sub-tab and not the other four or Job Displacement; renamed 2026-08-01, see [`adr/0005`](adr/0005-adoption-to-economy-rename.md). Not to be confused with **adoption** the variable, which is live and correct terminology throughout the charts and prose.
_Avoid_: Adoption (as the name of this group), Impact

**Adoption**:
The economic variable, not a part of the site: the share of firms reporting they used AI to produce goods or services, as measured by the Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey. Industries are cut into high, middle and low adoption groups for the payroll and turnover comparisons. Correct and current usage; the term survives in `assets/adoption-charts.js`, `src/styles/adoption.css`, the `.ad-*`/`#ad*` prefix and `analysis/src/exhibits/adoption_*.R`, none of which were renamed. Adoption is not causation, and the pages that use it say so.
_Avoid_: using it for the nav group (that is Economy), or as a synonym for AI's effect on employment

**Papers** (the tab; `#papers`):
The tab/view listing every entry of `RESEARCH_DATA`, one row per paper (a study, report or article), fifty to a page, with the search-and-filters bar above it and a pager below. Named Papers, not Sources, so it cannot be mistaken for a fact's *source*: the paper a Fact Bank card is quoted from (the byline and the "Source:" line on each card). It was called Papers, then briefly Sources (2026-11-11), then Papers again; `#sources` and `#research` still land here and are rewritten to `#papers`.
_Avoid_: Sources (as the name of the tab or of a row), Research (the nav group that held Papers and Fact Bank until 2026-11-11)

**Paper summary** (pilot):
What an open Papers row shows for a paper listed in `data/paper-summaries.js`: a one-sentence lede, a body paragraph, and optionally a "Take note" paragraph on how much weight the evidence bears, with a line saying whether it was written from the full text and whether anyone has reviewed it. It replaces that paper's key finding and bullets in the UI; the other papers keep theirs. Rules and shape: [`paper-summaries.md`](paper-summaries.md).
_Avoid_: abstract (the paper's own), takeaways (the older bulleted form)

**Filter bar**:
The search-and-filters bar at the top of the Papers list, the Fact Bank list and (under the toolbar) the Legislation tab: search and Sort in a row, a "More filters" toggle that holds every filter (with a badge counting what is on inside it), a result line and "Clear filters". One shared stylesheet (`src/styles/filterbar.css`); each tab brings its own facets and script. The bar is sticky on all three: it stays at the top of the window while the list, or the Legislation tab's map and tables, scroll under it. On the Legislation tab (which kept it still until the owner asked on 2026-11-12 for it to stick like the others) the toolbar sticks with it, the layers, the office toggle, the Show menu and Clear, in one wrapper (`#legSticky`), and a title that is jumped to is kept clear of both with `--leg-bar-h`; on the results layer the wrapper holds the toolbar alone.
_Avoid_: Controls (the CSS class `.controls` is the bar's root, not its name), sidebar (the Papers tab had one until the bar replaced it)

**Pager** (and **page**):
The row of Previous, page numbers and Next under the Papers list and under the Fact Bank list, which show at most 50 cards (`PAGE_SIZE`, `src/js/00-state.js`) at a time, and under the Legislation tab's bills table, which shows at most 10 bills (`LEG_PAGE_SIZE`, `src/js/60-legislation.js`), and its 2026 Midterm results table, which shows at most 10 states (`LEG_RESULTS_PAGE_SIZE`, the owner's request, 2026-11-12); a page is built when it is opened, so cards on pages nobody visits are never loaded. Every filter, search or sort change starts the list again at page 1, and a `#entry/<id>` link (a fact's View Summary) narrows the Papers list to its paper by putting the paper's title in the search box, as a source's facts link does for the Fact Bank. In the Fact Bank's default sort a source's facts are never split across two pages, so a page can hold a few fewer than 50. One stylesheet, `src/styles/pager.css`; the buttons are drawn by `renderPager()`.
_Avoid_: Load more, infinite scroll (the lists do not append; a page replaces the one before)

**Fact Bank**:
The tab/view (`#fact-bank`) listing short, citable, number-bearing facts extracted from tracker sources, one to three per source. Stored in `FACT_BANK` (and each fact's surrounding paragraph in `FACT_CONTEXT`, `data/fact-bank-context.js`, loaded when a card is first opened) (`data/fact-bank-data.js`), rendered in `#factBankArea`, each record tied to one tracker entry via `paperId`. A fact's source is whatever its `paperId` says, never the number inside its `kf-<n>-<m>` id: ids are opaque, and `kf-41-1` and `kf-41-3` belong to paper 228. In the default Most recent sort a source's facts sit together under one source header (`.g-head`, built by `buildGroups` in `src/js/80-fact-bank.js`); the two ranking sorts stay flat. Extraction is governed by the `fact-bank` skill (`skills/fact-bank/SKILL.md`). Formerly called "Killer Facts."
_Avoid_: Killer Facts, Killer Facts page/tab

**Policy** (or **Policy Intervention**):
One entry in `POLICY_DATA` — a specific proposed government or institutional response to AI's economic effects (e.g. Unemployment Insurance Reform, AI Dividend). Has a `category` (one of 10 granular areas) and a `level` (disruption stage it addresses).
_Avoid_: Policy area, category (when you mean the policy itself, not its grouping)

**Category**:
One of 10 granular policy groupings on a policy (`safety-net`, `labor-rights`, `tax-wealth`, `healthcare`, `work-structure`, `education`, `antitrust`, `housing`, `jobs`, `international`), defined in `POLICY_CATEGORIES`. Used for column coloring (`POL_CAT_COLORS`) and description text (`POL_CAT_DESCS`) in the Map view.
_Avoid_: Policy area, merged category

**Merged Category** (or **Policy Area**):
One of 4 top-level groupings — Economic Security, Labor & Worker Rights, AI Governance, Mitigating Harms — that the 10 raw categories roll up into via `CATEGORY_MERGE_MAP`, defined in `MERGED_POLICY_CATEGORIES`. These are the four pathways of `becky_brief.md`, in Becky's order, and that key order is the render order: the Policy Map's four rows top→bottom, and the Links graph's vertical bands (Economic Security top → Mitigating Harms bottom). Two departures from the brief are deliberate and documented at the definition: tax and ownership policy sits in AI Governance rather than Economic Security, and surveillance splits by setting (workplace → Labor & Worker Rights, consumer/public + civil rights → Mitigating Harms). Mitigating Harms is thin pending new policy cards; an empty merged category is skipped at render, not drawn blank.
_Avoid_: Category, policy group

**Policy Link**:
A `pairsWith` entry on a policy — `{id, why}` — meaning the referenced policy reinforces or amplifies this one. Currently authored topically/administratively (not yet aligned to ESP's theory of the case from `becky_brief.md`). Rendered today both as a text cross-link (`.pol-xref`) inside the policy detail modal, and as an edge in the Links graph.
_Avoid_: Connection, relationship, edge (edge is fine in code/rendering context, but "Policy Link" is the domain term)

**Rival Policy**:
A `competesWith` entry on a policy — a substitute or alternative approach, not a reinforcing one (e.g. UBI vs. Guaranteed Income). Currently stored in the data but not visualized anywhere.
_Avoid_: Competing policy, opposite policy

**Map view**:
The existing four-column Policy Map layout, one column per Merged Category, policies listed alphabetically within each.
_Avoid_: Grid view, columns view

**Links view**:
The new (spike/test-run) second tab within the Policy Map, showing policies as nodes in a hand-rolled SVG force simulation, connected by edges derived from each policy's Policy Links, with nodes gravitating toward their Merged Category's vertical band.
_Avoid_: Graph view, network view (Links is the user-facing tab label)
