The policy case

Buy a public capability.
Keep a public right to refuse.

Procurement can specify data limits, independent evaluation, appeal rights, and a real ability to change providers. Public compute and interoperability can widen who gets to build.

What are the alternatives and trade-offs?

Disclosure alone: makes a system visible, but does not transfer a decision right. A procurement contract: can bind a vendor, but needs enforcement capacity. A public option: can create alternatives, but requires durable funding and democratic governance.

Safety has two tracks. Everyday rights need remedies now. Frontier systems also need independent capability evaluations, incident reporting, and enforceable deployment limits for severe risks. This local exercise does not model catastrophic risk or estimate its probability.

The policy case

Negotiate the job.
Not just the tool.

Give workers advance notice, bargaining rights, paid learning time, and a way to contest automated decisions. Public investment can favor tools that extend expertise and service quality.

What this exercise leaves unresolved

Training alone does not determine who receives productivity gains. Bargaining changes the distribution of gains, but needs worker organization and enforcement. A shorter week at unchanged pay requires agreement about costs and coverage.

Time savings are not wage or employment forecasts. We assume a fixed task bundle, no verification overhead, no change in demand, and no price response. Real systems can add work or reduce quality. Automating a task can erode expertise and learning pathways even when it frees time.

The policy case

Make support arrive
before the damage compounds.

Automatic enrollment, rapid bridge payments, and income floors address different parts of a transition. A social wealth fund is a way to share returns over time; it still needs a payment rule that meets immediate needs.

Where cash helps, and where it cannot substitute

A lump sum can cover an immediate gap but can run out. Recurring support can provide continuity but must reach people on time. Wage insurance supports earnings after reemployment and does not fill every gap before it.

Funding, benefit interactions, eligibility, and appeals need explicit design. This exercise does not establish an optimal transfer or a causal effect on employment. Cash expands options; it does not replace job quality, worker voice, health coverage, or limits on harmful AI uses.

The connecting argument

Power before deployment.
A bargain at work.
A floor through change.

These are complementary choices. A check does not make surveillance acceptable. A seat at the table does not pay the rent. A public AI system still needs public accountability.
