# Glossary

The words this project uses for its own parts, and the ones to avoid.
Orientation and build instructions are in the root [`README.md`](../README.md).

**Nav**:
The row of five tabs in the site header, in three Nav Groups: Research (Fact Bank, Papers), Policy (Agenda, Landscape) and Government action (one tab, labelled Enforcement and Legislation, which holds the three sections Cases, Enforcement and Legislation). The site title links home to About, which is the front page: a short introduction, then a Research, a Policy and a Government action section, each opening with the question it answers and then what the dashboard offers for it (the Government action section was added on 2026-11-12, when the owner asked for About to carry the day's work: its copy is drafted for the owner to edit, where the intro, Research and Policy are the owner's own; the page opened on three door cards, one per tab, until that day). It was a flat row for a day (2026-11-11) and went back to groups at the owner's request, with Fact Bank ahead of Papers so that each group reads alphabetically. Legislation left the Policy group for a group of its own on 2026-11-12, also at the owner's request, and for an afternoon was two tabs, Bills and Use Cases, until Use cases went back into it as the third view of its toggle and was then removed altogether at the owner's request (it had been one tab with a toggle of three views before); that evening the group was named Government action and its first tab Legislation (the tab had been called Bills). Agenda, a placeholder, was added first in the Policy group the same day, also at the owner's request, and Enforcement, another, in the Government action group at the owner's suggestion that enforcement actions and key cases could have a tab of their own. On 2026-11-17 the owner preferred one tab for Cases, Enforcement and Legislation to the two tabs, Enforcement and Legislation, so the group became a single tab, labelled Enforcement and Legislation in the owner's words (the group label Government action sits under it), with a switch between three sections (see Government action).
_Avoid_: section (when you mean a tab)

**Nav Group**:
One of the three clusters in the site header: **Research** (Fact Bank, Papers; Soft Green), **Policy** (Agenda, Landscape; Warm Pink) and **Government action** (one tab, labelled Enforcement and Legislation; Warm Gold). A group's tabs sit above a rule and a small uppercase label in the group's accent colour (`data-group`, `--group-accent` in `src/styles/shell.css`). The About page's three sections take the same accents as a rule above each part, and the intro underlines the two unknowns that Research and Policy answer in their colours (`data-group` in `src/styles/policy.css`). A group needs a `--group-accent` line of its own in `shell.css` (without one it takes the green default; a test checks that each has one). A nav group is a label only: it has no view key, no route, and no state. The Economy group (Data Tracker, Job Displacement) is retired, see below.
_Avoid_: tab category, section (when you mean the group)

**Agenda** (the tab; `#agenda`):
A placeholder, the first tab of the Policy group (`#agendaArea`; alphabetical, so ahead of Landscape), added 2026-11-12 at the owner's request. Nothing is behind it: the masthead (`TAB_DESCS.agenda`) asks "What is our policy agenda?" and says "Coming soon.", and one note under it says nothing is here yet and points to Landscape. Replace the note and the masthead copy when there is an agenda. The policy agenda that sat above the Landscape list until 2026-11-12 was deleted from the repository that day; none of it is shown on this tab.

**Landscape** (the tab; `#policy`):
The second tab in the Policy group, after the Agenda placeholder (Legislation was the second until 2026-11-12): the list of policies (`#policyArea`). It had a North Star agenda above the list until 2026-11-12, when the agenda was removed. Its route is still `#policy` (and `#policy/<id>` opens a policy card), so old links keep working; only the nav label changed. Not to be confused with the `landscape` field on a policy card, which describes the bills and initiatives around that one policy.
_Avoid_: Policy tab, Map (the old names)

**Government action** (the group's one tab, labelled Enforcement and Legislation; `#viewGovernment`):
The one nav tab of the Government action group, since 2026-11-17: the owner preferred one tab for Cases, Enforcement and Legislation to the two tabs, Enforcement and Legislation, they had been. It holds four Sections, each a view of its own with an address of its own (`#all`, `#cases`, `#enforcement`, `#legislation`), under ONE masthead for the tab (the owner, 2026-11-18: "There shouldn't be a different header and blurb for each view"; see Masthead below), switched by four buttons at the start of the toolbar (`#govSwitch` in `src/views/60-legislation.html`: All, then the owner's alphabetical order, Cases, Enforcement, Legislation), with the Show menu right after them. The nav tab is marked for all four and opens the section that was open last (All at first, since 2026-11-18; Legislation until then). All holds everything and the other three hold their own data alone, on a map shaded by it, with a card that holds it alone. Each section is a view in the router (`VIEWS`), so the body carries the section's own `-view` class and there is no class for the tab itself. The place chosen in the Show menu is shared by the four (`legView.pinned`). The tab is labelled Enforcement and Legislation, in the owner's words, above the group label Government action; the label leaves out All and Cases. About describes it as its Government action section and links to each section, All first.
_Avoid_: tab (for a section); the Enforcement tab and the Legislation tab (they were tabs until 2026-11-17)

**All** (a section of Government action; `#all`):
The first section of the Government action tab and the one it opens on (the owner, 2026-11-18: "There should be an All view that's separate from the Legislation view. Currently it looks like legislation holds all the data"). It holds everything, in the owner's order ("2026 midterm results, bills, and then cases and enforcement"), in its card and on its page: the 2026 midterm results by state (a table at the head of the page, "All jurisdictions · 2026 Midterm results", ten states to a page, and the results block of the card), the bills (the Legislation table), the key cases and the enforcement actions (each list ten to a page). It shows the bills' controls (Status, the checkbox, the topic chips, the search, Sort), and the search and the chips narrow the cases and enforcement actions too, since a row's words and topics are the same vocabulary as a bill's; the place is shared with the other sections and narrows every list and the results table. Its map is shaded by items, the bills, key cases and enforcement actions added up per state (the one place they are counted together, said in the legend: actions and cases are otherwise never counted with the bills or with each other), in thirds of the states that have any; its state card has a block for each kind, key cases then enforcement actions, each ending with a "See more details below" link that pins the place and takes the reader to that list, wherever the place has rows of the kind (a place with none says None listed and has no link). Its Federal entry in the Show menu is "Federal (Congress, agencies and courts)". Its address is `#all`, with no place. Legislation, Enforcement and Cases show their own data alone.
_Avoid_: Overview, Home (it is a section, not a front page; About is the front page)

**Masthead** (the Government action tab's, `TAB_DESCS` in `src/js/70-render.js`; `govLede()` and `govMasthead()` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`):
The header and blurb at the top of the Government action tab. There is ONE for all four sections, the same whichever is open (the owner, 2026-11-18: "There shouldn't be a different header and blurb for each view"). The header is the question "What is government doing about AI?". The blurb is computed from the data: the active bills, the key cases and the enforcement actions, each counted on its own (words for small counts, digits once the largest is big), "about AI, federal and in the states, with the 2026 midterm results by state, according to" the results' source, and then how to use the page: "Use the switch to see everything together or one kind at a time, and hover a state or choose a place in the Show menu to narrow what you see." It does not change with the section or the filters. The cautions the Enforcement and Cases blurbs used to carry (an allegation is not a finding; a ruling is only what that court held) are in the note under each list's table.
_Avoid_: a masthead for each section

**Reset button** (`#legReset` on Government action, `#ppReset` on Papers, `#kfReset` on the Fact Bank; the shared look is `.reset-btn` in `src/styles/filterbar.css`):
The one way to clear a tab's search, order and filters (the owner, 2026-11-18, chose it from three sketches because the small Clear pill and the Clear filters link were hard to see; they are gone from all three tabs). It is always there at the right end of the toolbar, the same size as the Sort menu, solid navy with the count of what differs from how the tab opens ("Reset all (4)") and an outline otherwise ("Reset all"). Pressing it puts the whole tab back. On Government action: no jurisdiction, no filter in any section, the order back to Most recent, and the All view; the count is the jurisdiction, the search words, the status, each topic, the checkbox, the order if not Most recent, and the section if not All. On Papers: the search, the order (Newest first) and Methodology, Category and Region; on the Fact Bank: the search, the order (Most recent), Evidence, both rating minimums, each keyword and Foundational. Pressing it with nothing to reset says so (a live region), rather than doing nothing silently.
_Avoid_: Clear, Clear filters (the old controls), Sort (it is not a filter, but Reset does put it back)

**Legislation** (a section of Government action; `#legislation`):
The fourth section of the Government action tab (`#legislationArea`; after All, Cases and Enforcement; the tab opened on it until 2026-11-18, when All became the first section and the one it opens on; its route has always been `#legislation`; it was a tab of its own until 2026-11-17, and until 2026-11-12 the second tab of the Policy group, for part of that day labelled Bills): a map of the United States, with a card beside it, and a table of legislation under it. The map, the card and the controls above them (the toolbar, the topic chips, the search) are shared with the other three sections: one area, `#legislationArea` (see Government action). Pointing at a state shows its bills in the card (which holds the bills alone here: the results and what the other sections list are on All); clicking it, or choosing it in the Show menu, pins it and narrows the table. The states carry no letters of their own (pointing at one names it in the card), and the nine too small to point at each also have a small-state button beside the map. A bill is state or federal: Congress is the Federal entry of the Show menu and works like picking a state (there is no Federal button). The map is shaded by bills, in three steps, the thirds of the states that have bills, with the legend naming each range. The 2026 midterm results are by state on All, not here (in its card, as totals for each office in its overview, and in a table at the head of its page): they were a layer of their own, with a toggle, an office toggle that coloured the map and a table of every state, until 2026-11-18, when the owner asked for that view to go and the state by state data to stay. The midterm results come from the Associated Press's midterm results and from nothing else: the page is cited below the map, with the bills' sources. The bills are in `data/legislation-bills.js`, which `tools/legislation.py` generates (see [`legislation-sources.md`](legislation-sources.md)) and the page fetches the first time any of the four sections opens (the card in all of them counts from it, and the map on Legislation and All is shaded from it), adding the rows to `LEGISLATION_DATA`. Federal: our own list, found by `tools/congress_fetch.py` in GovInfo's bill-status data (public domain): every bill and joint resolution of the 119th Congress whose title names AI or a close relative of it, each with its official record (title, stage, latest action, committees) and the Congressional Research Service's summary where it has written one (else its official title); a bill whose title does not but whose CRS summary does is hidden behind the checkbox, as a state bill that only mentions AI in its text is, and a House bill and its identical Senate companion share a row. State: our own list, from Open States (which follows each legislature's own record): every bill since 2025 whose text mentions "artificial intelligence" (and, once their searches are finished, "deepfake", "digital replica" or "synthetic media"; `TERMS` in `tools/openstates_fetch.py`), shown with its official title, the legislature's own summary where it gives one, its subject terms, its latest action and a link to the legislature's page. Nothing is taken from NCSL. Bills that failed, were withdrawn or vetoed, or whose session ended without passing them are left out (a note says how many). A bill whose title and summary do not name AI, and which only mentions it somewhere in its text, stays out of every count, shading and table until the checkbox "Include bills that only lightly mention AI", beside the Status menu, is on; the bill then shows marked "Mentions AI only in its text". The toolbar's Status menu and that checkbox, the topic chips (always showing, above the search) and the search and Sort narrow everything counted on Legislation and on All (where they narrow the key cases and enforcement actions too): the map's shading, the card, the counts and the table, while the masthead keeps saying what is loaded (on Enforcement and Cases they are not shown and do not apply). The table is sorted by most recent last action unless Sort says oldest or jurisdiction. Under the table's title (the jurisdiction and the count, "New York · 8 bills", with no word Bills in it) a stats bar says where the bills in view stand (see Stats bar). The bills table shows ten to a page (`LEG_PAGE_SIZE` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`), with the pager the Papers list uses; the card beside the map gives the jurisdiction's stats (its bill count, and the stats bar's bar and each stage's count and share) and a link, "See more details below", that pins the jurisdiction and takes the reader to its table (only the cards that hold bills, on Legislation and All, have it, and only for a place that has bills: none where there is no data yet to show); it never grows taller than the map column, scrolling inside if it must (it lists no bills: the owner had the bill previews taken out of the cards on 2026-11-12). Puerto Rico, the one territory Open States covers, has bills but no shape on the map: the Show menu and the tables reach it. The state outlines are generated into `data/us-map.js` by `tools/us_map.py`. The masthead is the tab's, the same in every section (`govLede()` and `govMasthead()`). There is no About door.

**Enforcement** (a section of Government action; `#enforcement`):
The third section of the Government action tab (`#enforcementArea`, the area it shares with Cases and All): what agencies and attorneys general have done about AI. It lists the rows of `data/legislation-actions.js` (`LEGISLATION_ACTIONS`, generated from the Google Sheet's `enforcement` and `cases` tabs by `tools/actions.py`, never by hand; the row format is at the top of that file, the strict rules are in `content/README.md`, and `src/js/55-enforcement.js` draws it) whose kind is `enforcement`. It began as a placeholder tab on 2026-11-12 (the owner's suggestion that enforcement actions and key cases could have a tab of their own), was built on 2026-11-17 as "two tabs by kind" (Legislation: the bills and the midterm results; Enforcement: the agencies, attorneys general and courts), and became one section of the one tab that evening, at the owner's request, with the courts' rulings in a section of their own, Cases. While the file has no row of the kind the section is still the placeholder (one note says nothing is here yet; the masthead is the tab's and says nothing of it); the first row turns the list on, and the list, its count and the tab's masthead are then said from the rows. The first 14 rows went in on 2026-11-17, in one batch: each was checked against the agency's or court's own page, and none has yet been read by someone with legal training, which the file's own rule requires before an entry is live (a comment above the rows says so). The list has its own count, never added to the bills or to the cases, newest first; a status and an outcome are plain text, and each row links to the agency's or attorney general's own page. A Show menu, a search and topic chips (the same topics as the bills) narrow it. The PLACE is shared by the three sections: the Show menu is the Legislation menu, a place chosen in any is still chosen in the others (`legView.pinned`), and the address carries it (`#enforcement/texas`, `#cases/federal`; a place that is not one shows every jurisdiction and the address is rewritten). "Federal" is two bodies, so Legislation's entry reads "Federal (Congress)" and here it reads "Federal (agencies and courts)". The map's card on All gets a block for each kind (key cases, then enforcement actions: the count for the place, "None listed" never "none", no titles, and a "See more details below" link to that list wherever the place has rows), and on Enforcement it holds that block alone, but only once the file has a row. The section does not ask for the bills file. The About page's Government action paragraph says what the sections list; it changed with the first rows.

**Cases** (a section of Government action; `#cases`):
The second section of the Government action tab, after All (it shares `#enforcementArea` and `src/js/55-enforcement.js` with Enforcement; the owner's order after All is alphabetical: Cases, Enforcement, Legislation): what courts have decided or are deciding about AI, the rows of `data/legislation-actions.js` whose kind is `case` (a ruling, or a case still being decided, such as Mobley v. Workday; a government's own suit, such as the FTC's against Evolv, is an enforcement action, not a case). It works as Enforcement does, with its own count, its own placeholder note ("Nothing is here yet. Key cases will be listed here."), a note under its table that says a ruling is only what that court held, and the same Show menu, search and topic chips; a topic picked on Enforcement that Cases has no row for is dropped on the way across. Its address is `#cases` and `#cases/<place>`.
_Avoid_: Key cases as the section's name (that is the list's title, "All jurisdictions · 1 key case")

**Small-state button** (`#legTiles`, `.leg-tile`):
One of nine buttons beside the Legislation map, one for each state whose outline is narrower than 45 map units (`LEG_SMALL` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`): Vermont, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland and DC, north to south. It wears its state's colour and does what the state's shape does: pointing at it, or giving it the keyboard's focus, previews the state in the panel, and clicking it pins the state. It sits in a column at the map's right edge, and in a labelled row under the map on a phone (520 px and narrower). Which states get one is computed from the outlines, not listed by hand. The map's own states carry no letters: a label that fits one shape (Florida's) sat off-centre on another, and the panel already names a state when it is pointed at.
_Avoid_: tile or callout (the markup says tile; in prose, small-state button), label (the map has none), abbreviation on the map

**Topic** (Legislation filter; `LEGISLATION_TOPICS` in `data/legislation-data.js`):
One of the nineteen headings of the Legislation tab's filter chips (Hiring and work, Deepfakes and likeness, Federal preemption and so on), the places AI is used first and how it is governed after. It is our reading, not a legislature's or anyone else's list: the page assigns a topic when one of its words is in a bill's title, or two different words of it are in the bill's summary or subject terms. A bill can have several, and "No topic" finds those with none. Not to be confused with the Fact Bank's keywords.
_Avoid_: category (the Papers tab's word for a different list), tag, keyword (the Fact Bank's word)

**Active (bill)**:
A bill that has been introduced and has not failed (the owner's definition, 2026-11-12). Which stages count is the `active` flag on each entry of `LEGISLATION_STAGES` in `data/legislation-data.js`: introduced, in committee, passed one chamber, passed both chambers and enacted are active; failed is not. A bill with no stage cannot be judged and is not listed as active, and a note under the table says how many were left out and why.

**Stats bar** (Legislation tab; `#legBillsStats`, drawn by `legStatsBar()` in `src/js/60-legislation.js`):
The bar under the Bills section's title, added at the owner's request on 2026-11-12. That title is the jurisdiction and the count ("New York · 8 bills") and nothing else: the word Bills was taken out of it the same day, at the owner's request, and the jurisdiction and count were set at the size of a section title (20px bold); once the bills are loaded the count is always there, even as "0 bills". It says where the bills in view stand: one segment for each of the five active stages that has some bills (the stages of the Status menu, in its order: introduced, in committee, passed one chamber, passed both chambers, enacted), as wide as its share of the bills, the bar and its key sharing one line across the table (the owner asked on 2026-11-12 for the bar to run the length of the table and for the text to be on the bar's line): the key at the right on one line and the bar filling the rest, the key wrapping beside the bar on a narrower window and the two stacking below 860px, with the key giving each stage's count and its share of the bills in view as a whole-number percentage ("370 in committee (69%)"; the owner asked for the percentages on 2026-11-12). Each percentage is rounded on its own, so together they can come to a point or two either side of 100, and they are not forced to 100; a share under 1% reads "<1%" and one over 99% with other stages having some ">99%", so a stage with bills never reads as none and one that is not all of them never reads as all. It counts the bills the title counts, so it follows the jurisdiction pinned or chosen in the Show menu, the search, Status, the topic chips and the checkbox for bills that only mention AI, and it is empty (and hidden) when no bill is in view. It always counts all of those bills, not the page of ten the table is showing. A screen reader gets one sentence for the bar ("Where the 8 bills stand: 3 introduced (38%), 4 in committee (50%), 1 enacted (13%).", with "under 1%" and "over 99%" in words); the segments' hover text gives the stage, its count and its percentage. The colours are the map's greens, deepening with each stage, and navy for enacted, with a hairline between segments. It is drawn from the data each time the table is, so nothing in it is typed in.
_Avoid_: progress bar (it is not progress toward anything), chart, funnel

**Economy**:
A retired Nav Group (the middle of the three), holding Data Tracker and Job Displacement (`data-group="economy"`). Named for the question it asks, which the About lede states as "how the economy will absorb it." Formerly called "Adoption," which named only the Census BTOS sub-tab and not the other four or Job Displacement; renamed 2026-08-01, see [`adr/0005`](adr/0005-adoption-to-economy-rename.md). Not to be confused with **adoption** the variable, which is live and correct terminology throughout the charts and prose.
_Avoid_: Adoption (as the name of this group), Impact

**Adoption**:
The economic variable, not a part of the site: the share of firms reporting they used AI to produce goods or services, as measured by the Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey. Industries are cut into high, middle and low adoption groups for the payroll and turnover comparisons. Correct and current usage; the term survives in `assets/adoption-charts.js`, `src/styles/adoption.css`, the `.ad-*`/`#ad*` prefix and `analysis/src/exhibits/adoption_*.R`, none of which were renamed. Adoption is not causation, and the pages that use it say so.
_Avoid_: using it for the nav group (that is Economy), or as a synonym for AI's effect on employment

**Papers** (the tab; `#papers`):
The tab/view listing every entry of `RESEARCH_DATA`, one row per paper (a study, report or article), fifty to a page, with the search-and-filters bar above it and a pager below. Named Papers, not Sources, so it cannot be mistaken for a fact's *source*: the paper a Fact Bank card is quoted from (the byline and the "Source:" line on each card). It was called Papers, then briefly Sources (2026-11-11), then Papers again; `#sources` and `#research` still land here and are rewritten to `#papers`.
_Avoid_: Sources (as the name of the tab or of a row), Research (the nav group that held Papers and Fact Bank until 2026-11-11)

**Paper summary** (pilot):
What an open Papers row shows for a paper listed in `data/paper-summaries.js`: a one-sentence lede, a body paragraph, and optionally a "Take note" paragraph on how much weight the evidence bears, with a line saying whether it was written from the full text and whether anyone has reviewed it. It replaces that paper's key finding and bullets in the UI; the other papers keep theirs. Rules and shape: [`paper-summaries.md`](paper-summaries.md).
_Avoid_: abstract (the paper's own), takeaways (the older bulleted form)

**Filter bar**:
The search-and-filters bar above the Papers list, the Fact Bank list and the Government action area's tables: ONE structure on all three (the owner, 2026-11-18: "structured the same as enforcement and legislation"), up to three stacked blocks, each sticky under the one before. The toolbar (`.bar-toolbar`) holds the filters, always showing (there is no "More filters" dropdown on any of them), with the Reset button at its right end: Government action's section switch, Show and Status menus and checkbox; Papers' Category, Methodology and Region; the Fact Bank's Evidence, Political noteworthiness, Truth and Foundational. The chips (`.bar-chips`) come next where a tab has them: Government action's topics, the Fact Bank's keywords. The search bar (`.bar-search`) comes last: search, Sort and the result line. One shared stylesheet (`src/styles/filterbar.css`), whose `.controls` class every block carries; each tab brings its own facets and script. Each tab's script measures the live heights of the blocks into `--bar-toolbar-h`, `--bar-chips-h` and `--bar-h` (`syncBarStack` in `src/js/00-state.js` for Papers and the Fact Bank, `legSyncBarHeight` for Government action), which a jump to the list and the Fact Bank's pinned source headers (`--kf-bar-h`) clear. On a phone and on a window shorter than 761px nothing sticks and the Reset button is as wide as the row. The Government action tab's bars kept still until the owner asked on 2026-11-12 for them to stick like the others.
_Avoid_: Controls (the CSS class `.controls` is the bar's root, not its name), sidebar (the Papers tab had one until the bar replaced it)

**Pager** (and **page**):
The row of Previous, page numbers and Next under the Papers list and under the Fact Bank list, which show at most 50 cards (`PAGE_SIZE`, `src/js/00-state.js`) at a time, and under the Legislation tab's bills table, which shows at most 10 bills (`LEG_PAGE_SIZE`, `src/js/60-legislation.js`), and its 2026 Midterm results table, which shows at most 10 states (`LEG_RESULTS_PAGE_SIZE`, the owner's request, 2026-11-12); a page is built when it is opened, so cards on pages nobody visits are never loaded. Every filter, search or sort change starts the list again at page 1, and a `#entry/<id>` link (a fact's View Summary) narrows the Papers list to its paper by putting the paper's title in the search box, as a source's facts link does for the Fact Bank. In the Fact Bank's default sort a source's facts are never split across two pages, so a page can hold a few fewer than 50. One stylesheet, `src/styles/pager.css`; the buttons are drawn by `renderPager()`.
_Avoid_: Load more, infinite scroll (the lists do not append; a page replaces the one before)

**Fact Bank**:
The tab/view (`#fact-bank`) listing short, citable, number-bearing facts extracted from tracker sources, one to three per source. Stored in `FACT_BANK` (and each fact's surrounding paragraph in `FACT_CONTEXT`, `data/fact-bank-context.js`, loaded when a card is first opened) (`data/fact-bank-data.js`), rendered in `#factBankArea`, each record tied to one tracker entry via `paperId`. A fact's source is whatever its `paperId` says, never the number inside its `kf-<n>-<m>` id: ids are opaque, and `kf-41-1` and `kf-41-3` belong to paper 228. In the default Most recent sort a source's facts sit together under one source header (`.g-head`, built by `buildGroups` in `src/js/80-fact-bank.js`); the two ranking sorts stay flat. On a window wider than 640px a source header pins under the stacked bar while its facts scroll past; its rounded top corners are filled by a page-coloured `box-shadow` (`src/styles/policy.css`), because without it the square corners of the cards passing behind the header showed through them (the owner, 2026-11-18). Extraction is governed by the `fact-bank` skill (`skills/fact-bank/SKILL.md`). Formerly called "Killer Facts."
_Avoid_: Killer Facts, Killer Facts page/tab

**Policy** (or **Policy Intervention**):
One entry in `POLICY_DATA` — a specific proposed government or institutional response to AI's economic effects (e.g. Unemployment Insurance Reform, AI Dividend). Has a `category` (one of the granular groupings in `POLICY_CATEGORIES`). It once also carried a `level` (one of five stages of AI disruption, Repair to Transform); that field and its table were retired on 2026-11-17, after the card stopped showing it.
_Avoid_: Policy area, category (when you mean the policy itself, not its grouping)

**Category**:
One of 11 granular policy groupings on a policy (`safety-net`, `labor-rights`, `tax-wealth`, `healthcare`, `work-structure`, `education`, `antitrust`, `housing`, `jobs`, `international`, `civil-rights`), defined in `POLICY_CATEGORIES`. Used for the node colours and the legend of the Links graph (`POL_CAT_COLORS`).
_Avoid_: Policy area, merged category

**Merged Category** (or **Policy Area**):
One of 4 top-level groupings — Economic Security, Labor & Worker Rights, AI Governance, Mitigating Harms — that the 11 raw categories roll up into via `CATEGORY_MERGE_MAP`, defined in `MERGED_POLICY_CATEGORIES`. These are the four pathways of `becky_brief.md`, in Becky's order, and that key order is the render order: the Policy Map's four rows top→bottom, and the Links graph's vertical bands (Economic Security top → Mitigating Harms bottom). Two departures from the brief are deliberate and documented at the definition: tax and ownership policy sits in AI Governance rather than Economic Security, and surveillance splits by setting (workplace → Labor & Worker Rights, consumer/public + civil rights → Mitigating Harms). Mitigating Harms is thin pending new policy cards; an empty merged category is skipped at render, not drawn blank.
_Avoid_: Category, policy group

**Policy Link**:
A `pairsWith` entry on a policy — `{id, why}` — meaning the referenced policy reinforces or amplifies this one. Currently authored topically/administratively (not yet aligned to ESP's theory of the case from `becky_brief.md`). Rendered today as an edge in the Links graph. The policy detail modal listed it, with its `why`, under a "How it interacts" section until that section was removed on 2026-11-17; the `.pol-xref` links that remain are the `{{pol:id|text}}` ones written inside a card's own prose.
_Avoid_: Connection, relationship, edge (edge is fine in code/rendering context, but "Policy Link" is the domain term)

**Rival Policy**:
A `competesWith` entry on a policy — a substitute or alternative approach, not a reinforcing one (e.g. UBI vs. Guaranteed Income). Stored in the data and shown nowhere on the page now (the policy detail modal listed it under "How it interacts" until 2026-11-17).
_Avoid_: Competing policy, opposite policy

**Map view**:
The existing four-column Policy Map layout, one column per Merged Category, policies listed alphabetically within each.
_Avoid_: Grid view, columns view

**Links view**:
The new (spike/test-run) second tab within the Policy Map, showing policies as nodes in a hand-rolled SVG force simulation, connected by edges derived from each policy's Policy Links, with nodes gravitating toward their Merged Category's vertical band.
_Avoid_: Graph view, network view (Links is the user-facing tab label)
