03 / How we might show up

We are economic security experts. The target is still moving.

We don’t know the extent of the technology.

How capable it becomes, how quickly it spreads, or how work reorganizes around it.

We don’t know the extent of the economic harms.

The scale, timing, and distribution of displacement and lost bargaining power remain uncertain.

It is hard to hit a target that may not exist in the form we imagine.

Existing harms warrant action now. Uncertainty about scale is not a reason to wait.

Economic Security Project / Working discussion08 / 12

Presenter comments / 08

The object is not to make leadership more enthusiastic about AI. It is to establish public standards that make it possible to encourage a useful deployment, renegotiate its terms, or stop it. Each demand should name the relevant actor, an instrument, an outcome, and a condition for failure.

08 / Technology

Expand what people can do. Limit what can be done to them.

Access to expertise should widen. Power to surveil, manipulate, and impose risks should face enforceable limits.

Make the demand concrete

Public capacity and procurement should test useful tools against simpler software, more staff, and other alternatives.

Show what would count

More people can solve a real problem, on reliable and affordable terms, with authority to challenge harmful uses.

A reason to build useful capabilities and a standard for refusing harmful ones.

Economic Security Project / Working discussion09 / 12

Presenter comments / 09

The pitch to skeptical leadership: neither access nor innovation is an unconditional good. A capability that enables surveillance or imposes unacceptable risks can diminish public freedom. Proposed first case: navigating a public benefit, comparing AI assistance with simpler forms and human support. Measure successful access, errors, time, privacy, and effective appeal. This is a suggested research design, not evidence of impact.

09 / Work

An hour saved should improve a working life.

Workers should have the power to turn productive gains into better pay, more time, and greater autonomy.

Make the demand concrete

Bargain over deployment and the use of gains before the new workflow becomes fixed.

Show what would count

Track pay, actual hours, intensity, discretion, learning, and service quality together.

A faster task has not yet told us who benefits.

Economic Security Project / Working discussion10 / 12

Presenter comments / 10

The pitch to skeptical leadership: accepting that a task can become faster does not concede the distribution of its benefits. Compare the same capability under different workplace agreements. Separate measured task gains from firm and economy-wide outcomes. Shorter hours at unchanged pay require sustainable financing and sufficient gains; no automatic one-for-one conversion is assumed. Protect training pathways as well as current jobs.

10 / Economic security

No one should have to prove AI took their job to deserve security.

Guarantee the essentials through change. Give everyone a claim on greater prosperity.

Make the demand concrete

Dependable income protection, healthcare, and essential services, with public claims on gains where they materialize.

Show what would count

People can leave bad work, provide care, and learn without losing the essentials, including outside paid employment.

Build a guarantee that holds even if the AI boom disappoints.

Economic Security Project / Working discussion11 / 12

Presenter comments / 11

The pitch to skeptical leadership: a durable guarantee should not depend on a speculative abundance forecast or an administratively unworkable test of AI causation. Basic security requires broad, durable financing. Additional claims on extraordinary returns can expand provision but cannot be assumed to fund its floor. Cash, public services, social insurance, and ownership serve distinct purposes. Future analysis must specify population, eligibility, adequacy, costs, and financing.
