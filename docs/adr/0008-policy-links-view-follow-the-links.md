# The Links view follows the links from one policy, and the force graph is gone

**Status:** accepted (2026-11-24; the owner picked it from four sketches, in her words: it "starts from one policy and lets you browse outward: compatible neighbours on the left, competing ones on the right, with reasons below. Click any neighbour to move to it."). Supersedes [0002](0002-policy-links-hand-rolled-svg.md), the hand-rolled force graph.

**Context.** The Landscape's Links view was a force-directed graph of every policy ([0002](0002-policy-links-hand-rolled-svg.md), [0001](0001-policy-links-reuse-pairswith.md)). The data it draws is small and has a shape the graph hid: 30 policies, 29 recorded partnerships (`pairsWith`) that join them into a few separate groups and leave a few policies without a partner, and 6 recorded rivalries (`competesWith`). The graph was one tangle, coloured by the eleven finer categories while it was laid out in four areas, needed a pass of its own to keep its labels apart, and could not be read on a phone. On 2026-11-23 it was replaced for a day by a set of cards of linked groups (never committed); the owner asked on 2026-11-24 for the graph back, and then, the same day, for other ways to show how the policies link. Four working sketches were made on the live cards and shown to her: a board of readable tiles with a line between the linked ones, the four areas and the links between them, one policy at a time with its neighbours, and every link as a row of words. She chose the third.

**Decision.**

- One policy is in the middle, on a navy card that names it and its area and carries a button that opens it. The policies that work well with it (`pairsWith`) are a column on its left, the policies that compete with it (`competesWith`) a column on its right, each a tile (a bold title over its area, the area's colour as the dot before the title) with a line to the middle. Under them every neighbour comes again as a row, partners then rivals, with the authors' reasons (`why`) in full, a paragraph for each author when the two wrote different reasons.
- The two kinds differ by shape, never by colour alone: a solid outline and a solid line for works well with, a dashed outline, a dashed line and a dashed rule on the row for competes with. No red: the brand keeps Warm Red away from Soft Green, so the one cue is the dash.
- Clicking a neighbour, in a column or on a name in the list, moves to it. A **Path** remembers the last 12 steps and shows the latest 5; an earlier step is a pill that goes back to it and drops what came after; stepping to the policy you came from is a step back, not a loop. A menu, "Start from", begins a new path at any policy. The path lives in the page only: the view has no address (see below).
- The view opens on the policy with the most neighbours (partners and rivals, each counted once; ties by title), worked out from the cards, so no policy is named in the code and nobody has chosen which one is shown first.
- Where the three columns do not fit (the stage under 640px wide, which is a phone) they stack in the same reading order and the lines are not drawn; the outlines and the headings over the columns still say which is which. The lines are measured from where the tiles are and drawn in an SVG under them, redrawn when the middle changes, when the area is shown again and when the stage changes size. The page is zoomed 5% (`zoom` on the body), so the measured rects are divided by the zoom, as `syncBarStack` does.
- The links are read through Compare's index (`polCmpIndex`, `src/js/30-policy-compare.js`): a link written on one card counts for both, an id that is no policy or one that is archived is nowhere, and the order is a policy's own entries first and then the policies that name it. Links and Compare therefore cannot disagree about what is linked, and the words are Compare's (Works well with, Competes with, No recorded partners, No recorded competing policies). A pair that the cards record as both partners and rivals would be in both columns, because that is what they say; a test fails if the data authors both.
- The type and the surfaces are the site's own: the shared filter bar's menu and pills (`filterbar.css`), the card-title size (15.5px bold), the Map's policy tile, the dialog's label over the list, the notes size (13px) for the reasons, and the same focus ring and 44px touch targets Compare has on a phone.
- The graph code (`renderPolicyLinksGraph`, 275 lines, the `POL_CAT_COLORS` table, its styles and its five groups of checks) is deleted; it is in git at `b44908e`. `src/js/30-policy-links.js` is the new view.

**Consequences.**

- The reasons are on screen, which neither the graph nor the groups showed without a hover or a click. The cost is that the whole landscape is not in one picture: you see one policy's neighbourhood at a time. The other sketches (the board of tiles, the areas and the links between them, the list of every link) are saved in the shared notes folder, with their data and the program that arranged the board, and any of them could be added as another view or a mode of this one.
- Nothing is invented: every number, name and reason on the page is the cards' own. The data has no direction (the cards do not say which policy comes first), so there are no arrows and nothing orders the steps of a path except where the reader went.
- Done later the same day: an address for each policy (`#links/<policy>`), which lets a link open the view on it and the browser's Back walk the path, the way the Compare view's addresses do ([0007](0007-compare-pair-addresses-push-history.md); [0009](0009-links-policy-addresses.md)).
- Not done: a Featured label on the tiles (never asked), and a search box over the menu (a 30-item menu in four groups was thought enough).
- The checks (`tests/interactions.js`) work out what to expect from the cards with their own code (a link on either card, an id that is no policy, the order) and walk every policy in turn, so they hold whatever the authors record. They were broken on purpose 28 ways in scratch copies, each a way the view could plausibly go wrong (columns swapped, the policies that name a card left out, reasons dropped, the path's caps, the step back, the stacking, the focus, the status message), and each was caught. jsdom has no layout, so the lines are checked with a width given to the stage, and the look was checked in a real browser at 1240, 700 and 375px; the line ends were measured against the tiles' edges to within 0.05px.

**Update (2026-11-24, later the same day):** the view gained an address, a Back pill, Randomize and Refresh buttons and Copy link ([0009](0009-links-policy-addresses.md)). The "Start from" menu moved onto the switch's row (the owner: "in line with the views"), with the pills, and Copy Draft and Print were added ([0010](0010-landscape-switch-row-holds-view-controls.md)). The line of instructions under the switch ("Click any policy on either side to move to it.") was taken out at the owner's request, and the headings over the two columns became the dialog's own label (15.5px bold), as Compare's row labels are.
